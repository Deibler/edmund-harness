# Brief template

The 30-second contract you write before round 1. Lives at
`design-<slug>/brief.md`. Five fields — all optional, but anything you
skip will come back as a round-3 "actually, I wanted…"

```markdown
# Brief: <slug>

## Who it's for
Casey, screen-printing at Warwick.  (or: a friend's wedding invite, or: nobody in particular, it's just for fun)

## Destination
T-shirt (12in print, 1–2 colors), sticker (4in), social post, hockey banner, etc.

## Palette
Vintage navy `#1B2A49` on cream `#F4EDE2`.  (Lock two hex codes if screen print.)

## Style
Vintage collegiate, distressed.  (Or: clean & modern, surf shop, minimal line, etc.)

## Constraints
- Flat graphic on white — NOT on a shirt mockup
- Must be under 5 ink colors for screen print
- Include the text "OCEAN CITY" arcing top, "MARYLAND" arcing bottom
```

## Asking the user

Don't run the whole template as a questionnaire — it turns a fun
iteration into a form. Instead:

1. **Read the person file first.** Casey's already said he wants flat on
   white, navy-blue collegiate, distressed. Pre-fill from that.
2. **Ask one closed question** about whatever you genuinely can't
   predict. "Shark or dolphin this time?" beats "what style?"
3. **Silently write the brief** from what you infer + the user's answer.

## When to re-read the brief

- Round 3 is going sideways → re-read to check you're still on-brief.
- User says "let's try something different" → update the brief, don't
  just start generating.
- You're about to spend >30 seconds on a refinement → 5 seconds of
  brief re-read is cheaper than the wrong refinement.

## What NOT to put in the brief

- Per-round feedback — that goes in `notes.md`
- Tool choices (which model, which prompt) — those are implementation
- Anything that applies to all Casey designs forever — that goes in the
  person file, not the per-design brief
